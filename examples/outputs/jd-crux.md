# JD crux decode: VP Product, Developer Platform, Veridane Systems

Source: `examples/jd-vp-product-platform.md`. One line per top-6 bullet: the problem the
team cannot solve yet, then the crux (the one input without which the others produce
nothing).

1. Platform strategy across two segments. Problem: self-serve and enterprise run as separate products. Crux: one owner for the build path, not one more strategy deck.
2. More paid adoption and shorter waits. Problem: growth work keeps adding steps that slow builds. Crux: a shared measure of developer wait that both goals answer to.
3. One platform team. Problem: product, design and engineering hand work over walls. Crux: cohesion, a single backlog and shared goals, not more process between them.
4. Controls enterprise buyers accept the first time. Problem: security controls ship late and come back. Crux: security in the design review, not at the end.
5. Executive communication for accountability. Problem: accountability is not happening on its own. Crux: a visible system for who owns each part of the build path, plus an executive sponsor.
6. Build and grow the team. Problem: the leads are new or missing. Crux: hiring leads who can own a slice of the build path end to end.

Repeated words: "platform" appears nine times, and "build" or "builds" five times. Hypothesis: the hiring manager is circling one pain, several teams each own a slice of the same build path and nobody owns the wait.

Assumption: Veridane assumes developer speed and enterprise controls are a trade-off to be balanced; the better teams treat them as one product with one owner. The question to leave behind: who owns the wait?
