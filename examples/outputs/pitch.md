# Pitch: VP Product, Developer Platform, Veridane Systems (90 seconds)

I run the developer platform at Quillstone Cloud, and the bet I would bring to Veridane is the one we made there: the time a developer waits, not the feature list, is the moat.

At Quillstone we treated the platform as a line of business with its own revenue, not a cost center. We grew that line from $18M to $63M ARR and cut median build time 44% in 15 months, through 7 product leads in a 70-person org. We also rebuilt the build cache and moved release checks into one workflow tool.

The trade-off we held was build speed for every team over custom work for the largest accounts, because developers who wait churn. Every quarter we decided what to stop, so the org stayed focused on the two bets that moved revenue and wait time.

When a team is behind, I cut active projects before I add headcount; focus is usually the bottleneck.

Your job description says "platform" nine times, which tells me the teams are not yet operating as one. Is the bigger gap the decision about what the platform owns, or the way product, design and engineering share ownership of it?
